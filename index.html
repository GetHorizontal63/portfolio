DATA VISUALIZATION

← Back to Portfolio

The problem: Decision-makers across healthcare and government are asked to act on data they cannot see clearly; data buried in spreadsheets, fragmented across systems, or delivered in formats that require a technical interpreter to read.

The process: Engagements begin with stakeholder interviews to define the decisions each dashboard must support. Source data, including claims files, enrollment records, quality measure extracts, and operational logs, is profiled, cleaned, and modeled into a reporting layer. Deliverables are built in Tableau, Power BI, or custom web-based tools depending on the organization's existing stack, with self-contained VBA-driven workbooks available for environments without BI platform access.

Delivered outcomes: Population health dashboards tracking 30+ HEDIS quality measures with drill-down by region and provider. Risk adjustment visualizations mapping HCC score distributions and coding gap trends for plan-level strategy. Payment integrity scorecards surfacing anomalous billing patterns in near real time. Executive-facing economic and program evaluation reports produced through automated Python pipelines (Matplotlib, Plotly), replacing manual chart-building processes that previously consumed 20+ analyst hours per reporting cycle.

POPULATION HEALTH ANALYTICS

← Back to Portfolio

The problem: Health systems and public agencies are expected to improve outcomes across chronic disease, preventive care, utilization, and equity, but their data is often fragmented across disparate eligibility criteria, arbitrary quality measures, and community-level data sources. That fragmentation leads to delayed interventions, uneven resource allocation, and missed high-risk populations.

The process: Population health analytics engagements unify clinical, claims, and social determinants data into a single measurement framework. Work includes cohort design, risk segmentation, quality metric tracking (including HEDIS-style measures), utilization trend analysis, and geographic access modeling. Deliverables are built as executive-ready narratives and interactive visuals so decision-makers can move from broad reporting to targeted action at county, provider, and community levels.

Delivered outcomes: Earlier identification of high-need populations, clearer prioritization for outreach and care management, and reproducible reporting that ties performance to place. Food desert mapping in Southern West Virginia is one example within a broader portfolio focused on prevention strategy, access gaps, quality improvement, and program impact.

DATA ENGINEERING

← Back to Portfolio

The problem: Analysts spend the majority of their time on manual data preparation; copying files, reformatting extracts, and reconciling mismatched schemas, instead of producing the analysis the organization actually needs. Reporting timelines are measured in weeks when they should be measured in hours.

The process: Engagements start with a data audit: cataloging source systems, mapping file formats and delivery cadences, and identifying where manual steps can be eliminated. ETL pipelines are built in Python and SQL to ingest, validate, transform, and load claims data, enrollment files, and provider rosters into analysis-ready schemas. Orchestration is handled through Apache Airflow or cron-based scheduling. Reporting layers use stored procedures and materialized views in PostgreSQL or Snowflake. All pipeline code is version-controlled (Git), tested, and deployed through CI/CD tooling. Documentation is written so any team member can run, audit, or extend it without tribal knowledge.

Delivered outcomes: Reporting turnaround reduced by over 60% across multiple engagements. Manual data preparation eliminated for recurring deliverables. Reproducible, auditable pipelines that survive staff turnover. Cloud-native implementations on BigQuery and AWS S3 processing millions of records on automated schedules with built-in validation and error alerting.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

← Back to Portfolio

The problem: Organizations make high-stakes decisions, such as program funding, clinical interventions, and regulatory submissions, without the statistical rigor to know whether their data actually supports the conclusion. Anecdotal evidence is treated as proof. Correlation is reported as causation. Results would not survive external review.

The process: Each engagement is scoped around a specific decision: Does this intervention improve outcomes? Are billing patterns deviating from expected norms? Which factors most strongly predict risk? The appropriate method, including hypothesis testing, multivariate regression, survival analysis, time-series forecasting, or A/B experimental design, is selected based on the data structure and the evidentiary standard required. Analysis is executed in Python (SciPy, Statsmodels) or R depending on the deliverable format and client environment. Every result includes effect sizes, confidence intervals, and documented assumptions; not just p-values.

Delivered outcomes: Economic and compliance reports grounded in defensible statistical evidence, used for executive strategy and regulatory submissions at the federal level. Program evaluation frameworks that isolated intervention effects across workforce and public health programs. Multivariate models quantifying the impact of policy changes on healthcare utilization. Deliverables are presented as plain-language narratives built for decision-makers, not statisticians.

GIS

← Back to Portfolio

The problem: Organizations often have geospatial data but no reliable way to convert it into operational insight. Terrain, coverage gaps, and infrastructure risk are hard to evaluate when location data is fragmented across tools.

The process: GIS work begins with spatial data alignment: coordinate normalization, layer validation, and quality checks across shapefiles, raster data, and GPS logs. Analysis workflows are built to map coverage, trace network relationships, and detect geographic blind spots. Outputs are delivered as interactive maps and reproducible notebooks that support both technical teams and non-technical stakeholders.

Delivered outcomes: Clear, decision-ready mapping products for planning and field operations. Faster identification of terrain-driven constraints and high-value expansion zones. Repeatable spatial analysis pipelines that reduce manual mapping effort and improve confidence in geographic decision-making.

Blood Pressure Value Edit Integrity Analysis

← Back to Projects

Excerpt: This report analyzes 20,357 blood pressure readings from a health system's CY2025 ambulatory EHR that were edited after initial entry, testing whether the population of edits behaves like random correction noise or a systematic pattern. Across every level tested (system, specialty, department, physician, age cohort, and editor relationship) the same signature holds: edits are systematically lower than the original reading (mean systolic change −11.33 mmHg, Cohen's d −0.60), edits compress the value range and converge on round numbers, and the bias is stronger when the edit is made by someone other than the original recorder (68.3% of edits; effect size −0.72 vs. −0.60 overall). The pooled edited population flags CRITICAL on 11 of 13 statistical criteria.

Longitudinal Time-Series Analysis of Pharma Industry Payments and Provider Prescription Habits

← Back to Projects

Excerpt: This report combines a properly specified Granger-style causality test (trend-differenced, permutation-significance, bidirectional) with a manufacturer-concentration analysis of the same Sunshine Act payment data. System-wide, neither direction of the causality test clears significance (Payment→Rx p=0.589; Rx→Payment p=0.351); only a small, nearly symmetric subset of individual providers (8.7%/9.9%) shows an above-chance temporal relationship, pointing toward reverse-targeting rather than a causal payment effect. Separately, payment activity is sharply concentrated (top 5% of companies drive 65.8% of events), and providers most favored by one company are, per a 0.72 Spearman correlation, the same providers broadly paid by dozens of others - though a direct join shows the Granger-significant subset is not disproportionately drawn from these "hub" providers.

Provider Time Burden from Food & Beverage Payments

← Back to Projects

Excerpt: This report measures pure volume (not dollar value) of industry Food & Beverage payment contact per provider at a health system against national and two state-level benchmarks across the full 2018–2025 CMS Open Payments history. The system's providers average 15.6 F&B events per provider per year, 2.37x the national rate, and unlike peer groups, which plateaued near their pre-COVID baseline after recovering, the system's rate has climbed every year since 2022 to an all-time high in 2025 (1.91x national). The leading manufacturer, once its three name-variant records are combined, accounts for 17,165 events, almost double the next-largest (8,453).

Correlational Analysis of Industry Payments and GLP-1 Prescribing Among System-Affiliated Providers

← Back to Projects

Excerpt: This report tests association (not causation) between Sunshine Act payments and GLP-1 prescribing within a common June 2023–December 2025 window. Paid providers prescribe at nearly twice the rate of unpaid providers (27.7 vs. 17.6 Rx/month) and payment volume correlates moderately with prescribing volume (Spearman ρ 0.41–0.53); but the sharpest available test of a real influence mechanism, brand-specific loyalty by manufacturer, is weak and points in opposite directions for the two manufacturers with material payment volume (ρ −0.17 and +0.21). A before/after jump around each provider's first payment is fully explained by a placebo test using an unrelated date, since system-wide GLP-1 volume grew 60% over the same window regardless of payments.

Care Everywhere Document Pull Anomaly Analysis

← Back to Projects

Excerpt: This report screens every organization connected to a health system's Epic instance through Care Everywhere for anomalous document-pull activity, using 179 million completed pulls at daily grain (July 2023–June 2026). The most extreme outlier, a Medicare Advantage payor, shows a peak single-day z-score of 381.9 and a 31.6x increase in average daily volume, sustained since April 2025. The remaining organizations are grouped into active ongoing surges, resolved episodic spikes, connection lifecycle events, erratic high-volatility organizations, and large scale-driven non-anomalies. The report also includes a methodology correction showing the "% unspecified encounter" signal doesn't hold up once tested network-wide, plus demographic and age findings by organization.
